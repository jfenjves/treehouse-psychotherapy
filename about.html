About Jessica Fenjves, MHC-LP

As a mental health counselor, I work collaboratively with individuals to understand and ease painful patterns that may leave them feeling stuck or disconnected from themselves and others. My approach is psychodynamic and relational, grounded in compassion, curiosity, and a nonjudgmental regard for each person's experience. I draw upon mindfulness and somatic practices when appropriate, supporting greater awareness of the connections between mind and body. Through honest self-reflection and an exploration of the underlying sources of distress, I aim to help clients develop a deeper understanding of themselves, discover new perspectives, and experience greater freedom in how they relate to themselves and the world around them.

I hold a master's degree in Mental Health Counseling from the City University of New York and a master's degree in Art Education from Columbia University's Teachers College. I work with children, adolescents, and adults, bringing a creative and integrative perspective to my clinical work. My background in teaching and education informs my understanding that each person has a unique way of learning, growing, and coming to know themselves.

I am particularly interested in the inner life: dreams, fantasies, and the associative pathways the mind follows when it is free to wander. I value the process of becoming curious about our inner worlds and the ways we relate to others. My aim is to offer a thoughtful, supportive space where questions are welcomed, self-understanding can deepen, and new ways of seeing oneself and one's experiences can emerge.
